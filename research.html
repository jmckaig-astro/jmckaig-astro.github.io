Research

Turning line ratios into physics

Almost everything we know about gas surrounding an accreting supermassive black hole arrives as emission lines and absorbed continuum. My research makes that translation quantitative: photoionization and radiative transfer models detailed enough that a measured ratio becomes a statement about metallicity, density, ionizing spectrum, or obscuring geometry — not a qualitative impression.

The programme spans scales, from the sub-parsec broad line region through the coronal and narrow line gas out to the circumnuclear obscuring medium, and connects theoretical grids to data from JWST, SDSS, XMM-Newton, Chandra, and Keck.

Ultraviolet metallicity diagnostics for the broad line region

Broad-line metallicity estimates lean on a handful of UV ratios whose calibration is degenerate with ionization parameter, gas density, and the shape of the ionizing continuum. I am building radiation-pressure-confined CLOUDY grids to break those degeneracies and identify which ratios still carry metallicity information once confinement is treated self-consistently.

Current NPP fellowship focus.

Coronal lines and the role of dust

High-ionization coronal lines are among the cleanest signatures of an accreting black hole, yet they are far weaker in observed spectra than simple photoionization arguments predict. My work showed that depletion of refractory elements onto grains, together with absorption of ionizing photons by dust, can suppress coronal line luminosities by orders of magnitude relative to dust-free gas — so prominent coronal emission requires substantial grain destruction.

The ionizing continuum and accretion disk physics

Every emission-line model inherits its answer from the ionizing spectrum it is handed, and the standard thin disk does not reproduce the observed optical–UV–soft X-ray continuum. I am interested in where the thin disk fails, what observations actually constrain between the UV downturn and the soft excess, and how sensitive line diagnostics are to that uncertainty.

X-ray signatures of the circumnuclear medium

Mid-infrared interferometry showed that much of the dust around AGN lies along the polar direction rather than in an equatorial torus. Using ray-tracing simulations I quantified how that polar gas alters the X-ray spectrum, finding enhanced equivalent widths in the soft fluorescence lines and ruling out a filled-cone geometry for many Type 2 sources.

Emission-line diagnostics across the AGN population

Individual objects only go so far. I use large spectroscopic samples to test how line strengths scale with luminosity and accretion rate — the Baldwin effect in high-ionization lines, photoionization-based bolometric corrections, and the behaviour of coronal and narrow lines in low-metallicity environments where standard calibrations are least secure.

Finding accreting black holes that hide

Mid-infrared colour selection, coronal lines, and X-ray detection each miss a different part of the population, and in metal-poor dwarf galaxies compact nuclear starbursts can mimic every AGN signature we normally trust. I work with collaborators on JWST spectroscopy of these ambiguous systems, using photoionization modeling to separate genuine accretion from star formation.
