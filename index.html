About

Between the model grid and the telescope

I study how gas near supermassive black holes is heated, ionized, and obscured — and what its emission reveals about the accretion flow underneath.

I am currently a NASA Postdoctoral Program Fellow in the X-ray Astrophysics Laboratory at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, developing new ultraviolet metallicity diagnostics for broad-line AGN by combining radiation-pressure-confined photoionization models with archival spectroscopy.

I completed my Ph.D. at George Mason University with the Black Hole – Galaxy Connection group, working on ray-tracing simulations of the circumnuclear medium and photoionization models of AGN coronal line emission. Before that I earned a B.S. in applied physics and applied mathematics from Christopher Newport University.

What I do

Photoionization modeling

Large CLOUDY grids for the broad, narrow, and coronal line gas — built so the assumptions are visible and the outputs interpolate directly against measured line ratios.

See the grids

Radiative transfer

Monte Carlo and ray-tracing simulations of the circumnuclear medium, used to predict how obscuring geometry imprints itself on the X-ray spectrum.

MNRAS 512, 2961

Spectroscopy

Working with JWST and SDSS spectra, X-ray data from XMM-Newton and Chandra, and near-infrared observations from Keck to test those models against real sources.
